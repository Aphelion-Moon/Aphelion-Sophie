# If you are migrating from elsewhere...
You can bring familiar faces. They may need different luggage.

For SS 13 players arriving from **Bubber, Splurt, or Nova Sector**, we offer a preferences import utility.

Use `Export-Preferences` on your previous server. Once you have your Whitelist role and can enter Meridian Rift, use `Import-Character-Preferences` to import them. You will be disconnected after the import; reconnect to continue.

Preferences move settings. They do not move the setting.

# Leave the past in the past
This is a new community and an opportunity for new stories. Our servers have their own lore. Old heroes are not automatically the main subject of these new stories.

You can use characters from other servers, but change their backgrounds to agree with our lore. A familiar name does not bring an automatic claim to status, authority, or everyone else’s attention.

We recommend a reset of most previous character relationships. Give people room to try something different, even when they are wearing a familiar face.

# Don't assume your character knows their friends or enemies
When you meet a returning character, check with their player before assuming your characters know each other.

A quick message in **local out-of-character chat (LOOC)** is enough: *“Do our characters know each other in this setting, or are we starting fresh?”*

Keep the relationship only when both players agree. Otherwise, introduce yourself. No one owes you an old friendship, romance, rivalry, or grudge.

Leave old server feuds behind, too. A fresh start is not a request to ignore safety concerns; bring those to staff. It is a request not to make new neighbors inherit an old argument.

Board The Shuttle:
# You are not travelling alone
You are one of many seeking new experiences and new horizons. Other people are here for the same reason, and their time matters as much as yours.

**You are not the main character. None of us are.**

That does not mean your character is unimportant. It means other characters are important, too.

Give people something to respond to. Let their choices matter. Leave room for someone else to speak, act, succeed, or surprise you. A good scene is something you make together, not something you make everyone else watch.

# A shared seat is not an invitation into someone’s lap
Access to the same server does not entitle you to someone’s time, attention, or participation in a particular scene.

Ask rather than assume. Respect a refusal without making someone defend it. A previous yes is not a permanent yes, and a character relationship does not erase a player’s boundaries.

You can tell difficult stories without being cruel to the people making them possible.

# We crush sea-lions with shuttles
Genuine questions are welcome. Disagreement is welcome. Asking for clarification is welcome.

[Sealioning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sealioning) is something else: repeatedly demanding explanations or evidence in bad faith, refusing to engage with answers, and keeping someone trapped in a discussion they have already tried to leave.

Polite wording does not make harassment polite. You do not get to wear someone down and call it a conversation.

People do not owe you an endless defense of their identity, their boundaries, or their right to be here. Staff are open to feedback; that does not make anyone an unlimited debate service.

If you genuinely do not understand, say what is unclear. We would rather answer a sincere question than leave someone guessing.

Admire the lights:
# We are here to enjoy ourselves
At the end of the day, this community and these games are a hobby. Hobbies should bring some joy and energy into our lives, not consume everything we have left.

Do not improve your own experience by trampling someone else’s. Winning, being noticed, or getting the scene you imagined is not worth making another person regret logging in.

Take a break when you need one. You do not owe a hobby every evening you have.

# Every life is valuable, every experience is unique
Behind every character is a person who has lived a life different from yours. No one arrives knowing everything, and not everyone finds the same things easy.

Give people room to ask, learn, and correct themselves. A mistake is not automatically malice. Someone being new is not an invitation to make them the joke.

That patience goes both ways. When someone explains that you have hurt them or crossed a boundary, listen. Being willing to learn includes being willing to change what you are doing.

The games we play and the stories we create are chances to grow. Leave people better equipped for the next encounter, not afraid of it.

# Take photos and make memories
Keep the funny logs, the photographs, the videos, and the little moments you will otherwise forget. Share the things that made you glad you were here.

For memories you intend to share, get agreement from those involved before posting private conversations or sexual scenes. Keep personal information out of screenshots and recordings.

This does not prevent you from sending evidence to staff when you need help. Reporting a concern is different from putting someone on display.

Shared memories come from shared experiences. Make sure the people in yours are happy to be part of what you share.

# Watch your step and do not run
Take your time. Spend those extra two seconds considering the world around you.

These games have become routines for many people. Look beyond the fastest route to your usual equipment. 
Introduce yourself. Notice the person standing beside you. Give an unfamiliar situation time to become a story.

You have arrived somewhere new. Let it be new.

# Feedback is a two-way street
Staff and leadership help maintain this community. Your voice helps shape it.

Tell us when something is not working: the experience we provide, our services, a member’s behaviour, or our own decisions. Being responsible for the rules does not put us above feedback.

Use ** #open-a-ticket **, the leadership question channels, or public discussion as appropriate. 
Tell us what happened, what the problem is, and what you think would help. You do not need to have a finished solution before raising an issue.

We cannot promise every suggestion will become a change. We can do far less about problems no one tells us about.

# Be safe, Spacer
The world is a cruel place, and no one can prepare for everything.

Pay attention to the world around you. Look after the people sharing it with you, and remember that you are one of those people.

If something is wrong, say something. If you need help, reach out. Do not fade away or die silently.
